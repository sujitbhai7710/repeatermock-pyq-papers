# SSC CGL Maths — Top-Level Concept-Wise Taxonomy

**Purpose:** Master taxonomy for an SSC CGL mock-test/question archive. It preserves the 30-chapter, type-wise structure of the supplied **Aditya Ranjan SSC Maths 3rd Edition** index and adds a clearly marked **[SSC-EXTRA]** layer for SSC-relevant concepts missing as explicit headings in that index.

**Hierarchy:** `Chapter → Concept → Named Method/Shortcut → Question Pattern/Theorem → Exam metadata`

**Tags:** `[AR-3E]` = supplied Aditya Ranjan 3rd Edition index; `[SSC-EXTRA]` = additional SSC-relevant classification; `[THEOREM]` = theorem/property; `[METHOD]` = named preparation-method label.

> **Current SSC check:** The SSC CGL 2026 notice is the current reference for the exam structure; its Tier-II Paper-I includes Mathematical Abilities, while the syllabus covers number systems, arithmetic, algebra, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry, statistics/probability and data/graphs. The taxonomy below therefore keeps the book's organization but expands it where the official SSC scope requires finer classification.

# SSC CGL Official Coverage Cross-Check

The current SSC CGL 2026 notice should be treated as the authority for exam scope. Its Mathematical Abilities syllabus includes, among other items:

- Number systems; whole numbers, decimals, fractions and relationships between numbers
- Percentages; ratio & proportion; square roots; averages
- Simple and compound interest
- Profit and loss; discount; partnership business
- Mixture and alligation
- Time and distance; time and work
- Algebraic identities; elementary surds; graphs of linear equations
- Triangle and centres; congruence and similarity
- Circle, chords, tangents, angles subtended by chords and common tangents
- Triangle, quadrilaterals, regular polygons and prescribed 2D/3D figures
- Trigonometric ratios, degree/radian measures, standard identities and complementary angles
- Heights and distances
- Histogram, frequency polygon, bar diagram and pie chart

Therefore, **do not delete a concept merely because it is not a printed heading in the Aditya Ranjan index**. Keep the book hierarchy as the source taxonomy, then use the `[SSC-EXTRA]` layer to make the archive granular enough for actual SSC questions.

# Tier-I vs Tier-II

Use the same concept taxonomy for both tiers and add a `tier` field rather than duplicating chapters. This allows queries such as:

- Tier-I + Geometry + Circle + Tangent
- Tier-II + Percentage + Reverse Case
- Tier-I + Profit & Loss + Butterfly Concept
- Tier-II + Time & Work + MDH
- SSC CGL 2025 + BPT + Similarity
- Hard + Trigonometry + Maximum/Minimum

The 2026 SSC notice specifies **25 Quantitative Aptitude questions in Tier-I** and **30 Mathematical Abilities questions in Tier-II Paper-I**; use those exam-stage labels in the archive rather than treating them as different subjects.

# ONE PYQ → ONE CONCEPT RULE

## Mandatory rule

**The same PYQ/question must NEVER be assigned, copied, counted, or stored under multiple different concepts.**

Each individual PYQ must have:

```text
EXACTLY ONE PRIMARY CONCEPT
```

It may also have:
- a question format,
- a method/shortcut label,
- exam metadata,
- difficulty,
- source metadata,

but the **same question itself must not be duplicated into multiple syllabus concepts**.

---

## Example

Question:

> An article is marked 20% above cost price and sold at a discount of 10%. Find the profit percentage.

Correct classification:

```json
{
  "concept": "Profit & Loss",
  "subtype": "Markup + Discount + Profit",
  "method": "Percentage Method"
}
```

Do **NOT** store the same question separately under:

- Percentage
- Discount
- Profit & Loss

It appears **once only**, under:

> **Profit & Loss → Markup + Discount + Profit**

`Percentage` and `Discount` can be stored only as internal method/support tags if desired; they are **not separate concept copies**.

---

# PRIMARY-CONCEPT SELECTION RULE

When one PYQ contains multiple mathematical operations:

1. Identify the **final quantity/result being asked**.
2. Identify the **main concept that organizes the solution**.
3. Assign the question to that one concept.
4. Put supporting calculations into the `method` or `tags` fields.
5. Never create duplicate copies of the PYQ.
